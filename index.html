Air taxiswon't be the next Uber

Five years ago, air-taxi makers promised electric aircraft that would hop over traffic for about the price of a rideshare. We checked that promise against their own filings, outside studies and the history of flying people around cities. Here is what we found.

Three things to know first

Air taxi
Also called an eVTOL. A small electric aircraft that takes off and lands straight up, like a helicopter, using many small rotors.
Cost per passenger-mile
What it costs to fly one person one mile. It is how we compare air taxis with cars and helicopters.
Not advice
This page is our analysis and opinion, built on public sources. It is not investment advice.

1 · The promise

About the price of an Uber

In 2021 Joby and Archer told investors a seat would cost about $3 a mile, close to the $2.34 an UberX averaged at the time. Service would start in 2024. Rooftop pads would let you skip traffic.

2 · We have tried this before

Flying over traffic is not new

From the 1950s, helicopter airlines flew passengers from Manhattan rooftops and city heliports to the airports. New York Airways flew from the roof of the Pan Am Building to JFK. In May 1977 an accident on that roof killed five people, and rooftop service ended.

The idea kept coming back. A flying car flew in 1947. A "flying jeep" flew in 1962. Each time, the hard part was not the aircraft. It was noise, safety, cost and where to land.

In 2007 an air-taxi company called DayJet tried again with a new kind of small jet. It said it would break even at $2 a passenger-mile. An outside study put its costs closer to $3.30, and it went bankrupt in 2008. Its jet had been advertised at $775,000 in 2001 and was priced at $2.15 million by 2008. A 2007 government forecast expected 3,000 to 7,600 of these small jets to be delivered between 2016 and 2025. About 1,000 of the two leading models were built, and both are out of production.

NTSB AAR-77-09; Villa, Morejón Ramírez and Moore, "The Business Case for Regional Air Mobility at Scale," AIAA 2023-3295, pp. 5 to 6, citing GAO-07-1001 (2007) and Mane and Crossley, AIAA 2006-7734.

3 · The real price

Closer to $6.74 a mile

We rebuilt the cost of one air-taxi seat using Joby's own 2021 model and swapped in figures from company filings and independent studies.

That is the cost to run the aircraft, before any profit. An Uber is about $2 to $3 a mile. In 2023 Joby's chief executive told the AP that launch prices would be "a little north of $5 a mile."

To be fair, $6.74 is below what Blade charges for a helicopter seat to JFK today, so a premium airport shuttle can make money. The question is how big that market is. Blade's whole passenger business takes in about $145 million a year. That is a premium niche, not a mass market.

4 · The whole trip

The flight is the short part

An air taxi flies pad to pad. The US has only 49 public heliports that are not at hospitals, and fewer than 2 in 100 people in the 20 largest metro areas live within two miles of one.

So most trips start with a drive to the pad, then about 15 minutes of check-in, a short flight, and another ride at the other end. We timed it with real New York taxi data.

The air taxi saves about half an hour at rush hour and ten minutes overnight, at many times the price. That is the trip Blade already sells by helicopter, and it works because Manhattan already has heliports on the water.

America is built for the car

When the 1920 census first counted more Americans in towns and cities than in the countryside, the country already had 8 million registered cars. In 1956 Congress paid for the Interstate highways, about 41,000 miles of them.

An air taxi is not replacing a train. It is replacing a car the rider already owns, parked outside, that goes door to door. Every air-taxi trip still starts and ends in one.

New York is the exception, and it is where Joby and Archer plan to start. Only 29% of New York City commuters go by car. In Dallas, Phoenix and Charlotte more than 90% drive.

The next rival drives itself

Driverless taxis go door to door. At Waymo's published Las Vegas prices, a 20-mile trip costs about $54. The same trip by air taxi, with a ride to and from the pads, costs $105 to $132.

The federal government values ordinary travel time at $20.10 an hour, travel where flying is an option at $53.50, and business air travel at $86. On typical American roads the air taxi is slower for short trips, and no trip we tested pays off unless your time is worth at least $70 an hour. Only long trips in New York traffic come close, which is the airport market helicopters already serve.

5 · The aircraft

Upkeep nobody has measured

No air taxi has flown long enough in paid service for anyone to know what its batteries and maintenance really cost. As of October 2026 every company's number is a target, a model input or a lab test.

The one certified electric aircraft with a real fleet history is a small two-seat trainer, the Pipistrel Velis. Its official battery limit is 500 hours. A Dutch flight school told a trade magazine this year that its packs lasted about 230 hours before they could no longer finish a lesson.

Electric cars are the closest example, and they cut both ways. Routine maintenance did fall by about 40%. But the first batteries wore out early, and it took ten years of road data to know. Air-taxi batteries work much harder. They drain deep at high power several times a day and must still have full power to land.

7 · What probably happens instead

It survives, as something else

Our view is that the industry survives, but as something different from what was sold.

Defense

The Air Force, Army and Marines are already testing these aircraft, and military customers pay before a civil market exists.

Cargo and medical

Moving parts, blood and organs does not need a crowd of paying passengers.

A premium airport shuttle

A small number of high-fare routes in a few big cities, much like helicopters today.

Short-runway regional planes

Mark Moore, who led the engineering behind Uber's 2016 air-taxi plan and now runs Whisper Aero, has said taking off vertically costs four to six times more than using a short runway.

What we do not expect is a mass-market service priced like a rideshare.

8 · Where we could be wrong

The best case for air taxis

Governments may pay

Dubai and Abu Dhabi can fund the pads and accept losses.

Defense may carry them

Military work can keep the companies going until the civil market is ready.

Costs may fall faster

Mark Moore, who led the engineering on Uber's air-taxi plan, wrote in 2023 that it is "fairly clear" air taxis can reach $2 to $3 a passenger-mile at scale. That is a third to half of our $6.74. He gave no cost breakdown.

Joby is the strongest player

It is deep into FAA certification and well funded. One survivor can still do well.

Moore et al., "Unlocking Low-Cost Regional Air Mobility through Whisper Aero-Propulsive Coupling," AIAA 2023-3456, p. 6.

What would change our mind

  • A published battery life from aircraft in paid service
  • A route that runs full and profitable for a year
  • A city approving dozens of pads without a fight over noise
